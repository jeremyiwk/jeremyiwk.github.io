My work connects numerical methods with real-world instruments and imaging systems. I develop simulations, derive gradients, and tune the underlying linear algebra to make challenging physics problems practical to solve.

What I’m working on

At Metalenz, I work on computational photonics and metasurface design. This includes accelerating electromagnetic simulations on GPUs, building tools for gradient-based design, and optimizing imaging systems to be robust to fabrication errors and sensor noise.

How I got here

Previously, I was a computational scientist at Thermo Fisher Scientific, where I developed algorithms for focused ion beam alignment, differentiable charged-particle simulations, and models of ion beam milling.

I studied mathematics and physics at the University of Oregon, earning a bachelor’s degree in 2020 and a master’s in physics in 2022. My graduate research used molecular dynamics simulations to develop models of DNA and protein dynamics.
